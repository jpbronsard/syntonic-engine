## 0.2.5 (28 Oct 2026)

Bench and documentation only; the code is that of 0.2.0.

- Software bench, step 6e (`bench/software_2026-10-26/`, note version 1.6, section 15; rules and judge committed before the full run): the exponent of U' with power, in simulation on the generator of steps 6 to 6d (not an independent test). With a synthetic fractional gyro drift (H = 0.5, 0.6, 0.75, 0.9), 100 paired seeds and the optimum taken on the mean error over all seeds, the optimal exponent from nominal to x256 follows 1/(2H+1) (0.509, 0.465, 0.413, 0.369 against 0.500, 0.455, 0.400, 0.357; slope 0.976 [0.888, 1.072]), the square root is excluded at H = 0.75 and 0.9, and U' with H and lambda_H measured once at rest stays within 4.1 % of the optimum. Four of five hypotheses hold: the exponent's gain in error over the square root is resolved at H = 0.9 (20 % at x256) and not at H = 0.75, where the pre-registered hypothesis fails. The downward exponents fall below the prediction. Readings after the verdict (`step6e_exploratory.py`): the pairing of the bootstrap; the level of the optimum against U''s window; and the accelerometer noise of this generator, whose shape changes with the vibration level, which accounts for about 60 % of the downward offset at H = 0.5 to 0.75 in point estimate; with the shape accounted for, every downward exponent lies within its 95 % interval, and the rest is not resolved. The step-6e results commit and that script are first pushed with this release, after the commits of step 6f.
- Software bench, step 6f (note section 16; rules, then predictions frozen from a calibration, pushed to GitHub before the evaluation; design by the author): Theorem U'-Local, the local form of the law (a note whose deposit is pending), against the square root and against U' read with a raw local index, all fed the same structure function measured once at rest, with noise levels that keep the noise's shape. Seven of seven hypotheses hold on 200 held-out seeds, the validity check included: U'-Local's exponents match the measured ones within three standard errors in all 37 secants, through crossovers of the drift; its window is within 2.3 % of the optimum (regret at most 0.04 %); the raw reading overestimates the exponent at every secant of the two crossover drifts (by 0.015 to 0.053) and loses to U'-Local; the square root is far off; the optimum jumps where the drift's balance stops being monotone, between noise levels 2 % either side of the predicted one, by about the predicted factor (4.23 against 4.25, not judged); and with step 6e's noise the measured exponent follows the prediction that accounts for the noise's shape. In simulation on drifts drawn from the laws the theorem assumes: a test of the mathematics, the calibration chain and the code, not of a gyroscope.
- README: the attitude-estimator section carries steps 6e and 6f with their limits; one sentence in "What is claimed, and what is not". Bench README: run lines of steps 6e and 6f and the hash of the exploratory script. Version strings 0.2.5 (Python package, Rust crate, CITATION.cff).

## 0.2.3 (27 Oct 2026)

Bench and documentation only; the code is that of 0.2.0.

- Announcement at the top of the README: attitude estimation at Kalman parity with no tuning, pending the hardware test. Parity is in simulation (step 6b) with a two-state Kalman filter, the one-axis linear form of an EKF's fusion; it states what it is not: no hardware run of the law in an estimator, the Kalman core rather than PX4's EKF2 software, attitude only, one simulated axis, the drift fact needed and fragile when overestimated, slow learning of an uncalibrated gyro bias, and a few seconds of unsteady flight data.
- Software bench, step 6c (`bench/software_2026-10-26/`, note version 1.4, section 13; rules and judge fixed before the smoke run and committed before any result was written): a pre-registered ablation of the attitude-estimator law, in simulation on the generator of step 6b. With the same live noise estimate, the square root beats the best single fixed window chosen with hindsight over each run (total mean-square error 16 and 14 % lower) and exponents far from 0.5 (over the run with a x256 rise, 0 and 1 cost 3.8 and 5.0 times the total mean-square error); through that rise the law stays within 1.8 to 5.4 % of the clairvoyant Kalman and has 53 % less error than the fixed-noise one at x256. The best exponent over that run is 0.375 to 0.4 (grid minimum 0.375, 17 % below 0.5 in mean square; fitted 0.4005, at the edge of the band set in advance), and the predicted window fails its criterion at x256: 13.3 s against an optimum of 8.0 s, 1.65 times (limit 1.5) and a regret of 10.2 % (limit 10 %), attributed, untested, to the gyro's bias random walk acting through the bias integral tied to the window. Four of five hypotheses hold; the verdicts of the x256 run rest mostly on its x256 segment and carry seed noise.
- Software bench, step 7 (`bench/software_2026-10-26/`, note version 1.3, section 12): cross-axis coupling on logged data of the X500, predicted from the noise and drift matrices measured on the logs (no filter is run against a truth). The logs, reduced to the columns used (no position), are in `data/`; `run_step7.py` reproduces every result file. Step 7a (pre-registered), on the ground with the frame still: on the attitude estimator, roll and pitch, per-axis windows are predicted 0.58 and 0.82 % behind a two-axis Kalman filter in mean square (0.29 and 0.41 % RMS), under the 5 % build threshold; no matrix-law bench for the estimator. Step 7c (pre-registered): the gyro noise is uncoupled across roll, pitch and yaw (correlations at most 0.09); the estimate of the rate's random-walk intensity failed on all three logs, so its rules were not applied. Amendment A1 (exploratory): in the one flight logged at 1 kHz the motion appeared coupled (roll-pitch +0.76; the interval crosses zero without the last second), per-axis windows are predicted 8 to 11 % behind a three-axis Kalman filter in mean square (4 to 5 % RMS) and the symmetric matrix law within 0.3 to 0.4 % (0.2 % RMS); the TH44 collapse certificate points to yaw alone over the full window, not to a mixed direction. A point of theory: the matrix law's window is the best symmetric window, and the joint Kalman window is not symmetric when the matrices do not commute.

## 0.2.1 (26 Oct 2026)

Documentation only; the code is that of 0.2.0.

- The statement says what the law does not decide: the loop gain. The window law is invariant under rescaling of the error, so the gain is outside it. On a servo joint it lives in the servo's own loop and the engine only moves the setpoint. On a bare plant such as a quadrotor a proportional gain remains; the SyntonicQuad flew with no PID but with that gain set by hand (0.25, 0.25, 0.20; 0.90 from angle to rate), and the meta-gain law written for it (measured control effectiveness B, g = G* / B) has not flown. The authority fact is therefore the data-sheet speed and reaction time on a servo and the measured B on a bare plant, with G* among the design constants that must carry unchanged across machines. README, "What is claimed, and what is not"; CITATION.cff.
- What is common across substrates is the window law and its identification; where the first-order element sits (accumulated on a servo setpoint here, filtering the rate error under a gain on the quadrotor) is the substrate's, described by the authority fact.
- The Rust crate's version string, left at 0.1.1 in 0.2.0, now matches.

## 0.2.0 (26 Oct 2026)

- Identification on block means (the Allan-variance construction, scales 2 ms to 4.096 s, analytic noise floor n2 dt / l, constant C(H)), the new default `identification='allan'`. It fixes a limit of 0.1.x found by the software bench of 26 Oct 2026: with a noisy sensor or a slow motion, the signal falls below the read-noise floor at every lag up to 64 ms, the raw fit has no usable lag, H collapses and the window drifts by a factor of 2 to 3. With block means the engine lands within 7 % of the best fixed window in all 35 bench cells (fractional Brownian targets, H 0.30 to 0.95, optimal windows 5 to 160 ms).
- The same identification in the C++ header (`Config::identification`, `syntonic::ALLAN` / `syntonic::RAW`) and in the Rust crate (`Identification::Allan` / `Identification::Raw`), both default to block means. Replay against Python over 33 s and 12 joints: C++ 7.8e-16 rad, Rust 8.9e-16 rad with block means; 1.5e-14 and 5.4e-14 rad with the raw identification, unchanged. `make_replay_log.py` now writes both logs.
- `identification='raw'` keeps the 0.1.x arithmetic bit for bit (pinned by a unit test); `examples/servo_bench.py` and the raw replay use it, so the published tables and the earlier replay figures are unchanged. `servo_bench.py --allan` prints both identifications: in the servo regime the raw one stays a few percent better, mostly during the noise step (tip error ratios 1.02 / 0.78 / 1.07 / 0.91 against 1.00 / 0.69 / 1.07 / 0.89).
- New `C_H(H)` in the public API; new unit tests of the identification.
- The main statement changes. 0.1.x presented a joint correction loop for robots; 0.2.0 presents one formula with three machine facts and no tuning, at parity with hardened Bayesian Kalman banks within its class, and states the portability hypothesis (servo, quadrotor on a gimbal, human) with its pass criterion and its failure reading. `README.md`, section "What is claimed, and what is not".
- README: the validated domain of each identification, and the findings of the software bench against Bayesian Kalman banks (parity in stationary and slow-drift regimes; better after noise increases; behind after noise decreases and fast drift; 25 % behind a constant-velocity Kalman on smooth targets).
