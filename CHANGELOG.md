## 0.3.2 (2 Nov 2026)

The loop law's gain recomputation made cheaper in C++ and Rust in two ways that leave the chosen gains unchanged, with their Python reference. The gains, and so the commands, are those of 0.3.0 and 0.3.1 at every recomputation. The engine and the attitude law are unchanged. Not yet run on a microcontroller.

- **Two speed-ups of the gain recomputation** (C++ and Rust `LoopLawConfig::prune` and `quad`, both on by default; Python `ScanLoopLaw`, opt-in).
  - **Pruning.** Every term of a candidate's predicted loop error J is positive, so its partial sum never decreases, in floating point as in exact arithmetic. The candidates in use are evaluated first. Every 50 frequencies a candidate is dropped once its partial J exceeds, for every joint, the smaller of their J and the best J so far, times 1 + delta: it cannot be chosen.
  - **The quadratic form.** |1 + L|^2 = 1 + 2 Kp Re G + Kp^2 |G|^2 + 2 Ki Im G / W + Ki^2 |G|^2 / W^2, a quadratic in Ki with one coefficient per Kp row and frequency (P, filled as the row's first candidate reaches each block) and two per frequency (Q, R). |S|^2 then costs four operations and a division instead of ten and a division. It differs from |S|^2 computed from L by rounding only: at most 1.7e-15 relative on the replays' grids, and for any robust candidate (|1 + L| >= 1/2, |G| <= 1) at most about 1e-13, so about 5e-13 on J.
  - **Certificate and fallback.** A choice made with the quadratic form is kept when the next J exceeds the best by more than delta = 1e-9 in relative terms (`LoopLawConfig::delta`, a declared constant, at least 1e-10; a larger value costs time and never changes the gains). Pruning never drops a candidate within delta of the best, so the next J is known. Otherwise the scan is done again from L, exactly as in 0.3.1, and counted (`fallbacks()`). A NaN in the spectrum or the noise turns both speed-ups off for that recomputation.
  - **Python.** `python/syntonic_engine/loop_scan.py`: `ScanLoopLaw` is the loop law with its gains found by the scan of the C++ and Rust laws, with their arithmetic; its fallback is the frozen law's own matrix product. `loop_law.py`, the frozen law, is unchanged.
- **330 Hz servo frames.** Standard digital servos accept about 330 Hz at most, not 333 Hz. The Teensy sketches now set `cfg.frame = 1/330` and say so; the usage comments and docstrings note it. The library defaults stay at 3 ms, because the confirmed benches and the replays are built on it. The 333 Hz confirmations stay valid: 330 Hz lengthens the loop delay fact by 0.015 ms, far inside the ±1 ms latency circumstances the servo confirmation passed (0.971 and 1.009). ARM-1 will run at 330 Hz and give the law the frame it really uses.
- **The same gains** (`bench/scan_2026-11-02/`).
  - **Replays in C++ and Rust** (`cpp/tests/replay_laws.cpp`, `rust/examples/replay_laws.rs`). Every loop log of `rust/tools/make_law_replay_log.py`, now with a two-joint log of the realistic bench (RJ5 and RJ6, `replay_loop_bench2.bin`), is replayed five times: the speed-ups off, each one alone, both, and both with delta = 1e3, which makes every choice fall back. The gains are Python's at every tick in all five. There is no fallback in the first four. On every log the C++ and Rust laws evaluate exactly as many points as the Python reference.
  - **Python** (`scan_check.py`). At every recomputation of the logs, the scan chooses the candidate the Python law chose, in all four combinations, with no fallback.
  - **The gate** (`bench/goal_2026-11-02/package_gate.py all --scan`, new option `--scan`). With `ScanLoopLaw` in the frozen harness, all 90 servo runs of the confirmation are reproduced exactly (difference 0.0), and the 60 flights too. Over the 5733 recomputations, 43.8 % of the points were evaluated, 96 % at most, with no fallback.
  - **On an emulated Cortex-M7** (`bench/port_2026-11-02/m7/run_qemu.sh`, now with the speed-ups on and the two-joint log): the same gains as Python at every tick, with `-ffp-contract=off` and with GCC's fused default, the same points evaluated, no fallback.
  - **The closest tie.** At every recomputation of the replays the best J was below the next by at least 3.5e-6 in relative terms (from L and by the quadratic form alike), 3500 times delta; in the 90 confirmation runs at least 4.1e-7, 410 times delta (`package_gate_all_scan.log`).
- **Cost.**
  - **On our PC** (Xeon, g++ -O2, one core, the shortest of 5 replays per recomputation; `pc_timing.sh`). Two joints on the realistic bench (878 robust candidates): a recomputation took 4.2 ms in 0.3.1 and 0.84 ms in 0.3.2 (1.07 ms at most); pruning alone 1.1 ms, the quadratic form alone 1.9 ms. Six joints: 6.9 ms, then 2.4 ms.
  - **On a Teensy 4.1** (LLVM's Cortex-M7 model, 600 MHz; `m7/cycle_model.sh`, `cost_model.py`). A point costs 97 cycles from L (0.3.1, reproduced) and 70 by the quadratic form; filling P costs 42 cycles a frequency. Two joints with 878 robust candidates: a recomputation takes 0.215 s in 0.3.1; in 0.3.2, 0.160 s if nothing is pruned, 0.066 s on average and 0.085 s at most on the two-joint log. With 1246 candidates (no servo delay): 0.305, 0.225, 0.092 and 0.119 s. Assumed, as in 0.3.1: newlib's pow at 500 cycles.
  - **What pruning saves varies.** On average it evaluated 39 % of the points on the two-joint log, 47 % on the six-joint bench and 58 to 64 % on the closed loops. In single recomputations it evaluated up to 52 %, 67 % and 94 % (a closed loop with a disturbance step, where the two joints want very different gains), and 96 % in the confirmation runs. A per-tick budget therefore cannot count on it: with `spread` the work is divided as if nothing were pruned, and pruning only ends it early.
  - **The spread a per-tick budget needs** (two joints, 878 candidates; the law's own rule, `spread_for`). To stay within 0.45 ms of every 1 ms tick: 365 (0.3.1 needed 499 for about 0.45 ms). No spread below 500 stays within 0.25 ms. With 1246 candidates no spread below 500 stays within 0.45 ms either.
  - **What that spread costs** (`spread_budget.py`, as in 0.3.1: the realistic bench, development seeds 91-93, excerpts A and B, paired; described, not pre-registered). The tip error ratio to `spread = 0` at 365 is 1.025 at nominal and 1.033 under the noise step, worse in 6 of 6 each (0.3.1 at 499: 1.037 and 1.048).
  - **Not implemented: counting on pruning.** At the largest share seen on the two-joint log (51.5 %), the spreads would be 188 for 0.45 ms and 344 for 0.25 ms (ratios 1.006 and 1.010 at 188; 1.022 and 1.030 at 344). That would need a schedule that applies the gains later when pruning is poor, so their delay would depend on the data.
- **Scheduling helpers** (C++ and Rust, opt-in).
  - `time_candidate(clock, hz)` times one candidate on the board by the caller's clock (on a Teensy, the cycle counter).
  - `spread_for(seconds, budget)` gives the smallest spread for a declared time per tick, counted as above.
  - `set_spread(m)` changes the spread between recomputations.
  - Together they derive the spread from a measured cost and a declared budget: a scheduling choice, not a tuned parameter.
  - Also `points_evaluated()`, `points_total()`, `fallbacks()`, `late()` (a recomputation not finished before its last tick; only after a fallback) and `tie_margin()`.
- **Memory.** About 125 KB + 13 KB per joint, 150 KB for two joints (0.3.1: 114 KB): the quadratic form's per-frequency constants and one row of P.
- **Teensy.** `examples/teensy/LoopLawTiming.ino` prints on the board: the cycles of a recomputation with each speed-up off and on, and the points each evaluated; the time of one candidate and the spreads for 0.45 and 0.25 ms; the longest tick at the first. It sets `-ffp-contract=off` for the law itself (`#pragma GCC optimize`). Compiled for Cortex-M7 against stand-ins, not run.
- **Tests.** Python `test_loop_scan.py`: `ScanLoopLaw` against `SyntonicLoopLaw` bit for bit in every combination and with the fallback forced. C++ `laws.cpp` and `cargo test` (3 new): the same commands at every tick with the speed-ups off, on and forced to fall back; `delta` checked; the scheduling helpers.
- **Docs.** README: a note at the top, the C++ and Rust sections, the checks, the listing. `rust/README.md`, `bench/README.md` (`scan_2026-11-02/`). CITATION.cff: a sentence in the abstract.
- **Version strings 0.3.2** in the Python package, the Rust crate and CITATION.cff.

## 0.3.1 (2 Nov 2026)

The two laws of 0.3.0 ported from Python to C++ and Rust, with the same facts and the same arithmetic, and checked against Python on replays. Nothing in the laws changed; the engine is unchanged in all three languages. Not yet run on a microcontroller.

- **C++** (header-only, C++11, no heap allocation, no exceptions, no RTTI).
  - **`cpp/SyntonicLoopLaw.h`**: `syntonic::SyntonicLoopLaw<N, PIPE>` with `LoopLawConfig`; `begin(q0, w0, cfg)` returns false when a fact is out of range, `step(q, qd, a, b, send)`. 114 KB for two joints: keep it in a static.
  - **`cpp/RiccatiAttitudeLaw.h`**: `syntonic::RiccatiAttitudeLaw<L>` with `AttitudeLevel` and `AttitudeConfig`; levels by number, `begin(levels, q_b, T_pre, cfg)`, `step(g, have_a, a, level)`, `arm()`.
- **Rust** (`no_std`, no allocation): modules `loop_law` and `attitude_law`, `SyntonicLoopLaw<N, PIPE>`, `RiccatiAttitudeLaw<L>`. Missing facts panic, as the Python classes raise; the accelerometer sample is an `Option<f64>`.
- **New option `spread`** (C++ and Rust, off by default). The loop law's gain recomputation (about 1.3 million complex responses) runs in one tick with `spread = 0`, as in Python. With `spread = m` it is divided evenly over the m ticks that follow (blocks of 50 frequencies, the arithmetic in the same order), and the same gains take effect m ticks later. Its Python reference is `SpreadLoopLaw` in `rust/tools/make_law_replay_log.py`.
- **Equivalence with Python** (`rust/tools/make_law_replay_log.py`, `cpp/tests/replay_laws.cpp`, `rust/examples/replay_laws.rs`).
  - **The replays.** The loop law on the realistic MAESTRO 1 bench (6 joints, development seed 91, the noise step, 2.5 ms servo delay; with `spread` 0 and 400), two joints and one joint in closed loop. The attitude law on a synthetic flight and on the realistic flight bench (C5, development seed 300).
  - **Loop law.** Same gains as Python at every tick, in both languages. Largest differences relative to scale: commands 2e-16, A 2.4e-13, gamma 6e-14, Ki 6e-17 (the grid, one rounding). The best and the next J differed by at least 3.5e-6 in relative terms at every recomputation, so rounding cannot change the choice.
  - **Attitude law.** Difference 0.0 in the angle, the biases, the covariance and the noise readings, in both languages.
  - **On an emulated Cortex-M7** (QEMU mps2-an500, arm-none-eabi-g++ 13, newlib; `bench/port_2026-11-02/m7/run_qemu.sh`): the same. With GCC's default fused multiply-adds instead of `-ffp-contract=off`: the same gains, the attitude angle within 1.3e-17 rad.
- **Cost.**
  - **On our PC** (Xeon 2.8 GHz, g++ -O2, one core): an ordinary tick of the loop law 0.3 to 0.4 us for two joints. A gain recomputation 4 ms for two joints with 878 robust candidates (servo delay 2.5 ms), 6 to 8 ms with 1246 (no delay). The attitude law about 0.1 us per gyro sample.
  - **On a Teensy 4.1** (600 MHz, an estimate from LLVM's Cortex-M7 scheduling model on the code arm-none-eabi-g++ produces; `bench/port_2026-11-02/m7/cycle_model.sh`): 97 cycles per (candidate, frequency) point, so 0.21 s per recomputation with 878 candidates and 0.30 s with 1246. That is too much for one tick. With `spread = 499`, about 0.45 ms of each 1 ms tick for two joints; an ordinary tick about 10 to 20 us. Assumptions: the model's double-precision costs (multiply 4 cycles, multiply-accumulate 8, divide 27), data in tightly coupled memory, newlib's pow and log at a few hundred cycles each. `examples/teensy/LoopLawTiming.ino` measures it on the board.
  - **What the delay costs** (`bench/port_2026-11-02/spread_effect.py`, realistic bench, development seeds 91-93, excerpts A and B, paired; described, not pre-registered). Tip error ratio to `spread = 0`: 1.015 (250 ticks) and 1.037 (499) at nominal, 1.019 and 1.048 under the noise step; worse in 6 of 6 each time.
  - **Not implemented, measured.** Stopping a candidate's sum once it exceeds the best J so far gives the same gains and would evaluate 46 % (6 joints) to 56 % (2 joints) of the points (`bench/port_2026-11-02/prune_estimate.py`). Writing |1+L|^2 as a quadratic in Ki per Kp row changes it by at most 1.8e-15 relative and would cut the modelled cost per point from 97 to 70 cycles.
- **Tests.** `cpp/tests/laws.cpp`: the facts, the gains on the robust grid in a closed loop, `spread` giving the first gains unchanged exactly m ticks later, the attitude law's prediction and update against the textbook formulas, the arming covariance. The same in `cargo test` (9 new tests).
- **Teensy.** `examples/teensy/LoopLawTiming.ino`, a timing sketch for two joints by the cycle counter (compiled for Cortex-M7 against stand-ins, not run).
- **Docs.** README: a note at the top, the laws in the C++ and Rust sections, the checks, the listing. `rust/README.md`: the laws in the crate. `bench/README.md` names `port_2026-11-02/`. Docstrings of the Python package: the laws are no longer Python only.
- **Version strings 0.3.1** in the Python package, the Rust crate and CITATION.cff; in the CITATION.cff abstract, the sentence saying the laws are Python only now says they are in C++ and Rust.

## 0.3.0 (2 Nov 2026)

Two laws whose gains are computed from measured facts, added to the Python package: the syntonic loop law for a servo joint and the Riccati attitude law. Both confirmed pre-registered, in simulation. 0.3.0 contains all of 0.2.9.

- **The 0.2.9 GitHub release.** It was created on main before the 0.2.9 branch was merged, so its tag points to commit c7a4001 and its archive holds the 0.2.8 code. 0.3.0 contains every commit of 0.2.9: the engine options, the MAESTRO 1 bench of 1-2 Nov and the flight controller bench (entry below).
- **Python package (Python only).** The engine is unchanged in all three languages, and the new laws are not yet in Rust or C++.
  - **`SyntonicLoopLaw`** (`loop_law.py`).
    - **What it does.** A joint correction whose PI gains are computed every 0.5 s, as the gains that minimise the loop error predicted from statistics measured live, among the gains that keep the loop robust.
    - **Statistics.** The disturbance spectrum, from block means of the residual at 2 to 256 ms, and the read noise, from the two encoder reads.
    - **Robustness.** Phase margin at least 30 degrees, max |S| at most 2.
    - **Facts.** dt, the frame, servo_tau, w0, and the servo's delay measured once.
    - **Interface.** `cmd = law.step(q, qd, read_a, read_b, send)`.
    - **Provenance.** It is the frozen `bench/goal_2026-11-02/servo/syntonic_loop_law.py` (sha256 a7a234cc) with a provenance paragraph added to its docstring; the code is unchanged.
  - **`RiccatiAttitudeLaw`** (`attitude_law.py`).
    - **What it does.** A one-axis attitude estimator. Its state is the angle plus one gyro bias per level of a known input that changes the noise (on a multicopter, the throttle). Its gains are the Riccati gains of the model the facts describe.
    - **Facts.** Per level: s2, lam and T_rec. Also q_b, T_pre, dt and dt_accel.
    - **Noise reading.** The published live Hadamard reading, with one memory per level, floored at the fact.
    - **Before and at arming.** Stationary alignment before arming, then the arming covariance from the calibration's error.
    - **Provenance.** It is LAW_R of confirmation 3 (`ricv2.RicV`), rewritten as a self-contained single column. Its `step` and `arm` are those of the harness.
  - **New tests.** `python/tests/test_loop_law.py`: the package law against the frozen file bit for bit on a closed loop, the chosen gains on the robust grid, a regression value. `test_attitude_law.py`: the prediction and the update against the textbook formulas, the stationary alignment, the arming covariance, consistency with its own covariance on a synthetic flight, the per-level noise memory, a regression value.
  - **The gate.** `bench/goal_2026-11-02/package_gate.py`: inside the frozen harnesses the two classes reproduce the recorded runs with a difference of exactly 0.0.
    - Servo: run 'C1 A 71', all three columns; with `all`, the LAW column of all 90 runs.
    - Flight: LAW_R and LAW_Rqb4 for seeds 500-501 in C1 and C3; with `all`, all 60 flights.
- **Bench `bench/goal_2026-11-02/`.**
  - **Provenance.** Written and run by the assistant with two sub-agents; not yet reproduced by the author. Every frozen file is copied byte for byte.
  - **Contents.** `NOTE.md` with the sequence, the results, the verdicts and the limits. `replay_setup.sh`, because the frozen scripts name absolute paths of the working session.
  - **Goal set by the author:** parity with or better than a tuned PID and a tuned Kalman in every circumstance, servo frames at most 333 Hz, a realistic test.
  - **Servo** (`confirm_servo.py`, sha256 af4996a0, fresh seeds 71-75 x excerpts A and B).
    - **Bench.** A realistic bench written by a sub-agent: MAESTRO 1 baton arm, 1 kHz loop, 3 ms frames, PWM latency 2.5 ms, compliance, gear play, quantization. It reproduces `examples/servo_bench.py` bit for bit at the published plant.
    - **Rivals.** PIDs tuned at commissioning on the published 280-point grid.
    - **Held.** S1: parity with the PID given the same lead and frame-mean term in all nine circumstances, worst 1.009. S2: better wherever the law re-measures what changed (noise step 0.778, bursts 0.887, servo 35 ms 0.930, 18 ms 0.970, load 0.948). S3: better than the conventional tuned PID everywhere, 0.708 to 0.902.
    - **Described.** Less jitter than the PID in every circumstance.
  - **Flight, looped bench** (`confirm_flight.py`, sha256 6eac13e1, seeds 400-409).
    - **Failed.** F1 (vibration step 1.149 against the corpus's tuned Kalman), F5 and F8 (1.256, 1.251).
    - **Held as predicted.** F4 and F7: the steady-state law does not reach the throttle-scheduled Kalman in switching flights (about 2x). So the goal was not met on that bench.
  - **Flight, realistic bench** (`confirm_flight3.py`, sha256 181406c1, fresh seeds 500-509).
    - **The bench.** Amendments 3 and 4 were declared after that failure. Block-bootstrap replay of the logged records replaced the loops, which had made the 60 s tilt mean 5 to 8 times too precise at half throttle. The initial attitude is unknown.
    - **The law.** The Riccati form, its structure chosen on dev seeds 300-309.
    - **Held.** R1: parity or better with all three truth-tuned Kalman filters in all six circumstances, worst 0.985. R2: better in the ten claimed cells, 0.266 to 0.906. R3: the steady-state law is not at parity with the scheduled Kalman in switching flights, 1.762 and 1.681.
    - KF_sched3 and KF_full3 converged to the same filter.
  - **Of 14 predictions, 3 failed,** all on the looped bench.
- **Docs.** README: an announcement at the top, the two laws in the quick start and the checks, and the repository listing. `bench/README.md` names the new folder. `rust/README.md` says the laws are not in the crate yet.

## 0.2.9 (2 Nov 2026)

Engine: two options, off by default, the servo lead and the e-geodesic travel of the window. Bench: the MAESTRO 1 joint bench of 1-2 Nov 2026 and a one-axis flight controller.

- Engine (Python, Rust, C++), two options; with both off the arithmetic is that of 0.2.8 (the replay logs of `rust/tools/make_replay_log.py` and the output of `examples/servo_bench.py` are byte-identical):
  - **Servo lead.** The command adds lead x the planned velocity, and the engine's own servo and plan models see the same lead, so its residual stays exogenous. Python `lead='servo+delay'` (or a negative number), Rust and C++ a negative `lead`: servo_tau + loop_delay(dt, frame, sensor_delay), from facts the engine already takes; a positive number sets a fixed lead. It needs the planned velocity: Python `step(..., qd_plan=...)` (an error without it), Rust and C++ `step_lead(q, qd, a, b, frame)` (Rust's `step` panics if the lead is on; C++'s `step` applies no lead).
  - **Window travel.** Python `travel='e'`, Rust `Travel::E`, C++ `syntonic::TRAVEL_E`: theta = 1/tau relaxes exponentially toward 1/tau_target with time constant `travel_T` (default: the longest lag, 64 ms), the window taking the target at the first fit; `tau_rate` is then unused. The default `'rate'` is the published travel.
  - Rust: `Config` gained the fields `lead`, `travel` and `travel_t`; build it with `..Config::default()`. C++: `Engine::target(j)` reads the law's target window.
  - New tests in all three languages. The replay logs gain two files with both options on (`replay_log_lead.bin`, raw; `replay_log_lead_allan.bin`), and the replays a mode argument (`raw-lead`, `allan-lead`). Agreement with Python with the options on: C++ 2.4e-13 and 3.6e-14 rad, Rust 6.2e-14 and 1.3e-14 rad.
  - `bench/maestro_2026-11-01/engine_options_gate.py` checks that the options reproduce, with a difference of exactly 0.0 mm, the bench arms they come from (BASE and E64 of `transport_confirm.py`).
- MAESTRO 1 joint bench, 1-2 Nov 2026 (`bench/maestro_2026-11-01/`, self-contained on the model of `bench/cadence_2026-10-31/`; written and run by the assistant, not yet reproduced by the author; seven pre-registered scripts with their hashes, two earlier frozen versions that never ran, the exploratory passes and every result file; `NOTE.md` records which predictions held):
  - **The servo lead** (`eg732_lead.py`, `eg732_hold.py`): with the frames at the servos' ceiling (1.5 ms, standing in for 732 Hz) the lead servo_tau x planned velocity lowers the tip error by 10 % (L1 held). With the loop delay added, (servo_tau + D) x velocity, by a further 4 % at every frame (D3 held). The gap between slow and fast frames does not move (D1, D2 failed), and with any lead the e-geodesic cadence falls to the slowest frame, about 10 % behind the fastest fixed frame (L2, E2, E2L, D4 failed). Best configuration: 0.587 mm pooled against 0.834 for 333 Hz without the lead, classical engineering rather than the law.
  - **The adaptive window** (`window_vs_frozen.py`): 15 % better than the window frozen at its own mean without the lead, 48 % with it (W1 to W3 held). That rival is weak: against the best single fixed window, chosen on one seed and run on four others (exploratory), the adaptive window is 4.5 % better, 12 of 16, and slightly worse on excerpt A under steady noise.
  - **The window's travel** (`window_transport.py`, then `transport_confirm.py` on fresh seeds with a declared time constant of 64 ms, the longest lag): relaxing 1/tau (the e-geodesic of Dual Geodesy) beats relaxing tau, 19 % at 1 s and 3 % at 0.1 s (P2a, P2b held), then 2.9 % in 20 of 20 on fresh seeds (C2 held). Against the published travel: 3.9 % better pooled with fewer bad episodes, but better in only 11 of 20 runs (C1 failed on consistency).
  - **The size of the effect** (exploratory passes, then `confirm_dose.py` on fresh seeds): against the best fixed window for the same conditions, the law is 14.6 % better under a x100 noise step (K1 held, 10 of 10), 16.4 % with the e travel (K2 held), and 6.2 % better with the e travel under frequent bursts (K4 held); the e travel's gain over the published travel in bursts did not replicate (K3 failed, 1.2 % against a 5 % bar). Exploratory headroom: a clairvoyant two-window schedule bounds the gain; the published law captures 72 % of it under the large step; an oracle noise estimate on the wrong time scale makes things worse.
  - **The PID given the same lead and frames** (`pid_lead_fair.py`, fresh seeds 41-45, the PID re-tuned joint by joint on the published 280-point grid at the 2 kHz loop, 1.5 ms frames and the same lead (servo_tau + D) x velocity; requested by the author): the PID wins. Under steady noise its tip error is 29 % lower than the engine's (0.350 against 0.491 mm, 0 of 10 for the engine), under the x10 noise step the two are level (0.615 against 0.630); R1 to R4 all failed, and the lead helps the PID more (29 % against 17 %). Exploratory: tuned on the other excerpt the PID loses almost nothing (0.364 mm steady). What remains for the law here is that it needs no tuning: the PID's published gains carried to the new configuration are 33 % worse than its re-tuned ones.
  - **kappa** (`kappa_dyn2.py`, fresh seeds): on the published bench the best static factor on kappa_H is 1.0, regret 0 (Q1 held); with the lead and the faster frames it is 0.7, regret 3.6 % (Q2 held). A dynamic kappa learned from the tracking error by extremum seeking settles at 0.99 and 0.96 (the author's JP1 held), and one shared factor does as well as six per-joint ones (JP2 held); it costs about 1 % (D1 failed) and does not find 0.7 (D2 failed in that setting).

## 0.2.1 (26 Oct 2026)

Documentation only; the code is that of 0.2.0.

- The statement says what the law does not decide: the loop gain. The window law is invariant under rescaling of the error, so the gain is outside it. On a servo joint it lives in the servo's own loop and the engine only moves the setpoint. On a bare plant such as a quadrotor a proportional gain remains; the SyntonicQuad flew with no PID but with that gain set by hand (0.25, 0.25, 0.20; 0.90 from angle to rate), and the meta-gain law written for it (measured control effectiveness B, g = G* / B) has not flown. The authority fact is therefore the data-sheet speed and reaction time on a servo and the measured B on a bare plant, with G* among the design constants that must carry unchanged across machines. README, "What is claimed, and what is not"; CITATION.cff.
- What is common across substrates is the window law and its identification; where the first-order element sits (accumulated on a servo setpoint here, filtering the rate error under a gain on the quadrotor) is the substrate's, described by the authority fact.
- The Rust crate's version string, left at 0.1.1 in 0.2.0, now matches.
